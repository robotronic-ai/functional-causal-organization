## Claim L0 — causal realization

\[
A>0\Rightarrow\mathcal C\text{ exists}.
\]

Status: true but weak.  It follows almost directly from functionalism.

## Claim L1 — localization of variation

At fixed:

\[
\mathcal S,\mathcal G,\mathcal O,
\]

if:

\[
A(e_1)\neq A(e_2),
\]

then under four-family closure:

\[
\mathcal C(e_1)\neq\mathcal C(e_2).
\]

Status: exact conditional consequence of the closure ontology.

The empirical/finite support for the required invariances remains separate.

## Claim L2 — organizational factorization

\[
A=H_C(\mathcal C).
\]

Status: stronger than L1.

It additionally requires that admissible variation in
\(\mathcal S,\mathcal G,\mathcal O\) at fixed \(\mathcal C\) leave \(A\)
unchanged.

Existing FCI fixtures provide finite support, not a universal theorem.

## Claim L3 — compact-profile sufficiency

\[
A=H(C_F).
\]

Status for the current numerical profile:

\[
\boxed{\text{not established and false for the current AF indicator family}.}
\]

Exact typed-binding and joint-synergy counterexamples show that compact causal
summaries can collapse distinctions relevant to independent access
consequences.

## Claim L4 — AF identifies CF

Examples:

\[
\mathcal A_F>0\Rightarrow R>0,
\]

\[
\mathcal A_F>0\Rightarrow\Gamma>0,
\]

or:

\[
\mathcal A_F>0\Rightarrow\Delta_{\rm prop}>0.
\]

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{refuted on frozen finite witnesses}.}
\]

The AF battery can be maximized by causally different realizations.

This does not refute a relation between \(A\) and \(C_F\); it shows that the
behavioral indicator profile does not structurally identify that relation.

## Recommended manuscript consequence

Do not claim that the ontology proves:

\[
C_F\Rightarrow A.
\]

Do claim that the ontology motivates \(\mathcal C\) as the location of the
residual organizational variable, and that:

\[
C_F
\]

is a compact, falsifiable candidate for predicting the functional consequences
of access.

Its name is therefore a construct hypothesis, not a completed identification
theorem.
